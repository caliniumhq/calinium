# Calinium Typography System

## Purpose

Typography is the primary method of communication within every Calinium experience.

Before a customer notices colors, animations, or components, they read.

The Typography System defines how written content should establish hierarchy, guide attention, improve readability, and support purchasing decisions.

Good typography should feel invisible.

It should make information effortless to understand while allowing products and imagery to remain the primary focus.

---

# Design Principles

## Principle 01 — Typography Creates Hierarchy

Customers should immediately understand what is most important.

Typography should communicate structure before words are consciously read.

### Implications

- Headlines establish importance.
- Subheadings organize information.
- Body text communicates clearly.
- Captions provide supporting context.

Visual hierarchy should eliminate uncertainty.

---

## Principle 02 — Readability Before Style

Beautiful typography that is difficult to read is poor typography.

Legibility always takes priority over aesthetics.

### Implications

- Comfortable reading sizes.
- Appropriate line height.
- Generous spacing.
- Strong contrast.
- Predictable alignment.

---

## Principle 03 — Fewer Styles Create Stronger Systems

Every additional font weight or text style increases visual complexity.

Calinium favors restraint.

### Implications

- Limit font weights.
- Reuse established text styles.
- Avoid decorative typography.
- Build consistency through repetition.

---

## Principle 04 — Typography Supports Products

Typography should enhance product presentation rather than compete with it.

Large headings should create confidence, not distraction.

Body copy should inform, not overwhelm.

---

## Principle 05 — Consistency Builds Trust

Typography should behave consistently across every page.

Customers should never need to relearn hierarchy.

Every heading, paragraph, button, and label should follow predictable rules.

---

# UX Foundations

## Miller's Law

People can only process a limited amount of information at once.

### Application

- Break long content into sections.
- Use descriptive headings.
- Keep paragraphs concise.
- Avoid large blocks of uninterrupted text.

---

## Hick's Law

More information increases decision time.

### Application

- Prioritize essential copy.
- Remove redundant wording.
- Present information progressively.

---

## Jakob's Law

Users expect familiar reading patterns.

### Application

- Left-align long-form text.
- Maintain conventional heading structures.
- Preserve recognizable ecommerce layouts.

---

## Gestalt Principle — Proximity

Related information should remain visually grouped.

### Application

- Consistent spacing between headings and paragraphs.
- Product titles remain visually connected to prices.
- Labels remain associated with their controls.

---

## Serial Position Effect

Customers remember beginnings and endings.

### Application

- Strong opening headlines.
- Clear closing calls-to-action.
- Important information appears first.

---

## Cognitive Load

Typography should reduce effort.

Customers should scan before they read.

Good hierarchy allows users to understand a page within seconds.

# Typography Roles

## Display

Used for major editorial moments.

Examples:

- Homepage hero
- Campaign banners
- Brand storytelling

Should be used sparingly.

---

## Heading 1

Primary page heading.

Only one H1 should exist per page.

Communicates the page's primary purpose.

---

## Heading 2

Major content sections.

Creates primary structure.

---

## Heading 3

Subsections.

Organizes supporting content.

---

## Heading 4

Component-level headings.

Cards.

Product information.

Feature lists.

---

## Heading 5

Minor section headings.

Navigation groups.

Sidebar titles.

---

## Heading 6

Small supporting headings.

Used only when additional hierarchy is required.

---

## Body Large

Long-form editorial content.

Stories.

Articles.

Brand philosophy.

---

## Body

Default reading text.

Product descriptions.

General information.

Policy pages.

---

## Body Small

Supporting information.

Metadata.

Helper text.

Secondary descriptions.

---

## Caption

Low-priority information.

Image captions.

Product specifications.

Legal notes.

---

## Label

Forms.

Inputs.

Navigation labels.

Filter titles.

---

## Button

Interactive actions.

Buttons should remain highly readable.

Avoid excessive letter spacing or decorative styling.

---

## Price

Pricing deserves its own semantic role.

Prices should remain immediately identifiable without overwhelming the surrounding layout.

# Typographic Hierarchy

Every page should establish hierarchy using:

1. Size
2. Weight
3. Spacing
4. Position

Hierarchy should not rely primarily on color.

---

# Line Length

Readable text should maintain comfortable line lengths.

Very long lines reduce comprehension.

Very short lines interrupt reading rhythm.

Body content should prioritize comfortable scanning over maximum width.

---

# White Space

Typography depends on surrounding space.

Whitespace is part of the typography system.

Every heading requires sufficient breathing room.

Dense layouts reduce perceived quality.

---

# Responsive Typography

Typography should adapt naturally across devices.

The relationship between headings should remain consistent regardless of screen size.

Scaling should preserve hierarchy rather than simply shrinking text.

---

# Accessibility

Typography must satisfy WCAG 2.2 AA.

Requirements include:

- Readable font sizes.
- Sufficient line height.
- Strong color contrast.
- Clear focus indicators.
- Zoom compatibility.
- Responsive scaling.

Typography should remain readable at 200% browser zoom without loss of functionality.

---

# Merchant Customization

Merchants should configure typography through semantic roles rather than individual components.

Customization includes:

- Heading font
- Body font
- Scale
- Font weight
- Letter spacing
- Paragraph width

Changing a typography role should update every component using that role automatically.

---

# AI Guidelines

When generating themes, AI should:

- Establish clear hierarchy.
- Minimize cognitive load.
- Preserve consistent spacing.
- Favor readability over decoration.
- Use typography to guide purchasing decisions.

Typography should never become the visual focus.

Products should.

---

# Future Compatibility

New typography roles require clear justification.

Before introducing a new role, ask:

- Does an existing role already solve this?
- Does it improve readability?
- Does it reduce cognitive load?
- Does it improve hierarchy?
- Does it remain consistent across the design system?

If not, reuse the existing role.

The Calinium Typography System values clarity, consistency, and timelessness over visual novelty.
